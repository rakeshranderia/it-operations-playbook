# Technology Roadmap

## Overview

A technology roadmap translates business strategy into a structured plan for technology investment, capability development and operational improvement.

A good roadmap should show not only **what technology will change**, but **why the change is required and what business outcome it is expected to deliver**.

---

## Roadmap Principles

A technology roadmap should be:

- Business aligned
- Outcome focused
- Risk based
- Financially realistic
- Prioritised
- Measurable
- Flexible

---

## Strategy to Execution

A practical roadmap connects:

**Business Strategy**

↓

**Business Capabilities**

↓

**Technology Capabilities**

↓

**Initiatives**

↓

**Investment**

↓

**Business Outcomes**

---

## Roadmap Categories

Technology initiatives can be grouped into several categories.

### Run

Maintain reliable and secure existing services.

Examples:

- Infrastructure
- Applications
- Support
- Security
- Licensing
- Maintenance

### Improve

Improve existing capabilities.

Examples:

- Automation
- Process improvement
- Service management
- Data quality
- User experience

### Transform

Create new capabilities or materially change how the organisation operates.

Examples:

- Cloud transformation
- Digital platforms
- AI adoption
- Core system replacement
- Business process transformation

### Protect

Reduce technology and business risk.

Examples:

- Cybersecurity
- Resilience
- Disaster recovery
- Identity
- Compliance
- Technology lifecycle

---

## Initiative Prioritisation

Technology initiatives should be assessed using a consistent framework.

Consider:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Strategic Alignment | Does it support business strategy? |
| Business Value | What outcome will it create? |
| Risk Reduction | What risk will it address? |
| Customer Impact | Will it improve customer outcomes? |
| Complexity | How difficult is implementation? |
| Cost | What investment is required? |
| Dependency | What other capabilities are required? |
| Urgency | What happens if we do nothing? |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identity Modernisation | Protect | High | 0–6 months | Reduced access risk |

## Roadmap Horizons

A practical roadmap can use three horizons.

### Horizon 1 — Stabilise

Focus on:

- Operational reliability
- Security
- Risk
- Technical debt
- Critical vulnerabilities
- Resilience

### Horizon 2 — Optimise

Focus on:

- Automation
- Efficiency
- Data
- Service improvement
- Platform consolidation

### Horizon 3 — Transform

Focus on:

- New business capabilities
- AI
- Digital transformation
- New operating models
- Strategic technology investments

---

## Technical Debt

Technology roadmaps should explicitly account for technical debt.

Technical debt may include:

- Unsupported systems
- Legacy platforms
- Outdated infrastructure
- Poor integrations
- Manual processes
- Unsupported software
- Skills dependencies

Ignoring technical debt can increase future cost and operational risk.

---

## Investment Planning

Technology investment should consider the complete lifecycle cost.

Consider:

- Implementation
- Licensing
- Infrastructure
- Integration
- Support
- Security
- Training
- Change management
- Future replacement

The cheapest implementation is not necessarily the lowest-cost option over its lifecycle.

---

## Roadmap Governance

The roadmap should be reviewed regularly.

Review factors include:

- Business strategy
- Budget
- Risk
- Technology changes
- Regulatory requirements
- Project delivery
- Vendor changes
- Emerging opportunities

The roadmap should be treated as a living management tool rather than a static document.

---

## Executive Perspective

A technology roadmap should enable leadership to answer:

> **Where are we today?**

> **Where do we need to be?**

> **Why do we need to change?**

> **What will it cost?**

> **What risks are we reducing?**

> **What business outcomes will we achieve?**

The ultimate purpose of a technology roadmap is to turn technology investment into measurable business outcomes.
