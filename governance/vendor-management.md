# Technology Vendor Management

## Overview

Modern IT environments rely on a combination of strategic partners, SaaS providers, managed services, consultants and specialist technology suppliers.

Effective vendor management should balance:

**Service + Risk + Cost + Performance + Strategic Value**

The objective is to manage suppliers as part of the broader technology operating model rather than treating vendor management as a procurement activity.

---

## Vendor Lifecycle

A practical vendor lifecycle is:

**Identify → Assess → Select → Contract → Onboard → Manage → Review → Exit**

---

## 1. Identify

Understand:

- Business requirement
- Technology requirement
- Service criticality
- Expected outcomes
- Internal capability
- Existing supplier dependencies

---

## 2. Assess

Suppliers should be assessed according to their risk and strategic importance.

Consider:

- Security
- Privacy
- Financial stability
- Service capability
- Business continuity
- Regulatory requirements
- Geographic dependencies
- Subcontractors
- Data handling

---

## 3. Select

Supplier selection should consider more than price.

Evaluation criteria may include:

- Capability
- Service quality
- Security
- Commercial model
- Strategic alignment
- Scalability
- Integration
- Support
- Exit considerations

---

## 4. Contract

Contracts should establish clear expectations around:

- Scope
- Service levels
- Pricing
- Security
- Privacy
- Data ownership
- Incident notification
- Business continuity
- Audit and assurance
- Subcontracting
- Termination
- Exit assistance

---

## 5. Onboard

A structured onboarding process should establish:

- Service ownership
- Contacts
- Access
- Security requirements
- Service levels
- Reporting
- Escalation
- Documentation
- Business continuity arrangements

---

## 6. Manage

Ongoing supplier management should include:

- Service performance
- SLA performance
- Incidents
- Financial performance
- Security
- Risk
- Relationship management
- Continuous improvement

---

## 7. Review

Strategic suppliers should be reviewed regularly.

Review areas may include:

### Performance

- SLA achievement
- Incident trends
- Service quality

### Commercial

- Spend
- Contract utilisation
- Pricing
- Value delivered

### Risk

- Security
- Compliance
- Business continuity
- Financial stability

### Strategic

- Innovation
- Roadmap alignment
- Future capability
- Relationship health

---

## Supplier Tiering

Not all suppliers require the same level of management.

| Tier | Description | Management Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Strategic** | Critical to technology or business strategy | Executive relationship + regular review |
| **Critical** | Significant operational dependency | Formal governance + risk monitoring |
| **Standard** | Important business service | Routine service management |
| **Low Risk** | Limited business impact | Proportionate oversight |

---

## Vendor Consolidation

Vendor consolidation may provide benefits such as:

- Reduced cost
- Simplified governance
- Improved accountability
- Reduced technical complexity
- Stronger strategic relationships
- Improved negotiating position

However, consolidation should not create excessive dependency on a single provider.

The objective should be:

**Simplification without creating unacceptable concentration risk.**

---

## Exit Strategy

Critical suppliers should have a realistic exit strategy.

Consider:

- Data portability
- Data destruction
- Alternative suppliers
- Transition time
- Knowledge transfer
- Licensing
- System dependencies
- Contractual obligations
- Internal capability

A supplier relationship should not become so embedded that the organisation cannot realistically leave.

---

## Executive Perspective

Effective vendor management is about maximising the value of external technology capability while maintaining control of organisational risk.

The key question is not:

> "How many vendors do we have?"

It is:

> **"Do our suppliers provide the right capability, at the right cost, with an acceptable level of risk and dependency?"**
